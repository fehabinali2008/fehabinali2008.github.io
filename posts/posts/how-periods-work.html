Demystifying the Flow: How Your Menstrual Cycle Actually Works

The menstrual cycle is a highly coordinated monthly internal communication system between your brain and your reproductive organs. Driven by fluctuating hormones, its primary job is to prepare your body for a potential pregnancy. When pregnancy doesn't happen, the uterus safely sheds its internal lining, creating your period.

While the textbooks always talk about an exact 28-day cycle, real-world data shows that fewer than 25% of individuals actually have a rigid 28-day schedule. A normal cycle can range anywhere from 21 to 35 days, and variations of a few days from month to month are completely healthy.
